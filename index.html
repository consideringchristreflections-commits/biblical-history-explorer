About this Explorer

Considering Christ begins with what Scripture actually says, preserving its setting and sequence. We then place independent historical witnesses alongside it—not to decide the conclusion for you, but to make the evidence accessible.

Sometimes the evidence corroborates details of the biblical account. Sometimes it raises questions, appears to contradict a claim, or is silent. We will not conceal disagreement or insist that one explanation has been established when the evidence remains unsettled.

Here is what Scripture says. Here is what the historical evidence says. Here is where they agree, differ, and where questions remain. Examine the sources and evaluate for yourself.

How to explore

  • Timeline: Scroll through historical years and pinch to zoom on a trackpad. Click a record to open the supporting research.
  • Atlas: Choose a historical scene, use the map controls to zoom and drag, and click a site to see its record.
  • What Was Known? Explore what people could have observed or heard at a particular point without assuming later knowledge.
  • Changing kingdoms: Use the 735, 732, and 722–720 BC comparison to see the political changes while the geography stays in place.

Our research commitments

We distinguish biblical testimony, contemporary or later historical sources, archaeological evidence, scholarly interpretations, and open questions. We label uncertain dates, suggested locations, and approximate political boundaries. New evidence may require revision.

We do not reproduce archaeological photographs. Where appropriate, we provide links to museums, excavations, and other publications so that you can examine the materials at their source.

Scripture translation

Unless otherwise indicated, Scripture reading links use the World English Bible (WEB), Protestant 66-book edition. It is a public-domain translation. Research references to a different translation or study edition are separately identified and are not the default reading text.
